The Problem

One initiative.
Different priorities. Unclear decisions.

“The business expects one outcome. The technical teams are delivering toward another. Who brings the decisions together?”

An initiative can have meetings and status reports while business and technical teams work from different assumptions. Budget, staff time, and leadership attention are consumed without a shared view of what matters or who decides. FIS creates the governance between these groups: shared priorities, accountable owners, evidence for decisions, and a clear escalation path.

  • 01

    Priorities without shared ownership

    Business outcomes and technical responsibilities are disconnected. Teams need agreed priorities, named owners, and clear escalation paths.

  • 02

    Decisions without a clear path

    Work stalls when evidence, approval responsibilities, and decision gates are undefined.

  • 03

    Status without executive visibility

    Leadership needs one evidence based view of progress, risk, dependencies, and the decisions required from both business and technical teams.

How We Define Completion

Day 1: agree the baseline.
Day 30: verify the handoff.

At kickoff, we confirm the initiative boundary, available evidence, participants, and acceptance criteria. The handoff provides leadership with the following basis for a decision; final executive decisions remain with the client:

Risks are visiblePriority risks and blockers are documented with evidence, status, and next actions.
Ownership is explicitAccountable owners and escalation paths are recorded; unresolved ownership decisions are clearly flagged.
Decisions have structureOutstanding decisions identify the responsible decision maker, evidence needed, and applicable stage gate criteria.

Completion criteria and timing assume timely access to the agreed information and stakeholder participation. Material changes are reviewed together.

Post Stabilization Governance Retainer
