Before we begin

A clear starting point.

What does our team need to provide?

A named sponsor, access to the budget approver, the agreed plans and project records, access to the relevant people, and time to check findings. We agree the number of sessions, evidence limits, response dates, and acceptance criteria before the delivery period begins. Delays or material changes are documented and the schedule is reviewed together.

What should be clear by Day 30?

Leadership receives documented risks and next actions, named owners or clearly flagged ownership gaps, open decisions and the evidence they need, and a recommended path forward. The client retains final decision authority. The written scope defines how the handoff is accepted.

What work is outside the engagement?

The engagement covers the agreed review, decision structure, and handoff. It excludes software development, daily project management, emergency response, repairs, integrations, and implementation. Any future AI system requires its own scope, price, and agreement.

How are findings checked and information handled?

AI can help compare records, flag gaps, and draft summaries using tools and handling arrangements approved for the engagement. Christopher checks findings against the evidence and stakeholder context and remains accountable for the final work. Leaders retain decision authority. Any NIST review is not certification.

Can an IT provider or adviser make an introduction?

Yes. If a client has a project with slipping dates, unclear ownership, or conflicting updates, a conversation can establish whether a focused review would help. Agree any client introduction and information sharing with the client first.
